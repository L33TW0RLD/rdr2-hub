## Invitation

Take a short countryside ride and learn to **read wildlife rather than just shoot it**. Even without bringing back a useful pelt, the excursion teaches observation, animal quality and the care required for good hunting.

## Organic sequence (not a checklist for progress)

1. **Look and listen.** Observe an animal; use available game prompts to study or identify it, paying attention to species and quality when visible.
2. **Choose rather than rush.** If the player wants to try a clean kill, check the animal's starting quality and the weapon recommendation shown by the game; do not assume the correct tool is owned.
3. **Learn from the result.** Compare the animal's final quality and inspect any resulting materials. A three-star starting animal does not guarantee a three-star pelt without a suitable clean kill.
4. **Transport thoughtfully.** Check actual horse carrying options before loading a whole carcass or pelt; the current horse's cargo and storage state are not known.
5. **Return naturally.** When content, the player can ride back to camp, consider Pearson's **visible** options or simply continue exploring. Never claim that a material was donated or credited until reported.

## Discovery allowance

A passing rider, unusual location or interesting animal may become the outing's focus. The Hub follows the player's curiosity with a spoiler-light response; no hard-coded surprise pins, mandatory material quota or loss of project progress.

## Practical guardrails

- Protect rare/unique materials if actually encountered; choose recipient only after establishing the item's purpose and available services.
- Don't claim a guaranteed species/quality spawn or that a certain ammunition type is owned.
- No blanket timer for decay or fabricated horse-carrying capacity.
- If a specific **player-confirmed** active Gold/missable stage becomes time critical, return to Mission Control instead of continuing the optional hunt.

**Why this outing can succeed without loot:** the player learns animal inspection, quality checking and how to plan a deliberate field action.
