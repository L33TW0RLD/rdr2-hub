### Default field principle (safe, usable during play)

**See something curious → approach carefully → identify its actual interaction → inspect or read if offered → consult journal/map → collect named evidence only if actually available → decide whether to pursue the story or leave it mysterious.** A picturesque place is not necessarily a counted Point of Interest or qualifying shack. A journal drawing is not proof that a treasure puzzle has been solved or its reward taken. [D26-C01–C07]

## 1. Definitions and evidence surfaces: six distinct verbs

D26 documents **world discovery**. It must not equate the geography of arrival with *an action performed* or with *a separate completion criterion*. The following classifications are **research categories**, not player progress flags. [D26-C01–C06]

| Verb / state | What the player really did | Appropriate evidence | Does **NOT** by itself prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VISIT / APPROACH** | Rode/walked to a landmark, cabin, clearing, ruin, rock face or unusual scene | Player-described location, map/location screenshot, local observation | POI registered; shack credited; letter physically obtained; puzzle solved |
| **ENTER / SEARCH** | Crossed into an accessible interior and looked around | On-screen interior and interactable objects, **Miscellaneous → Shacks** counter if applicable | Every building is a qualifying shack; all clues read; unique loot preserved |
| **INSPECT / EXAMINE** | Used a context-specific action on an eligible object/POI target, potentially triggering the observation/camera and sketch | Visible **Inspect/Examine** prompt → animation → journal or drawn map mark; exact in-game wording controls | All nearby objects were inspected; a linked quest started; a secret puzzle solved |
| **DRAW / RECORD** | Arthur/John created a notebook sketch or annotation (automatically in response to appropriate activity, not a freeform player drawing command) | Matching **Journal** page; annotated world map is secondary | A generic Compendium/Total Completion checkbox advanced; letter obtained |
| **READ / PICK UP / RETAIN** | Read a local note or physically acquired a named letter/book/map/valuable when the game permits | Item-specific Documents/Satchel record, inventory screenshot, readback; not all readable props are stored identically | Journal POI credit; clue chain decoded; physical item guaranteed in loaded save |
| **SOLVE / CLAIM / FINISH** | Used clues/objects to trigger an observable puzzle state, took reward or completed a named quest strand | Opened compartment or scene change; confirmed named pickup; mission/Total Completion/Compendium row **only if applicable** | All mystery lore explained, all related environmental clues discovered, save recorded |

**The split matters:** a point of interest is typically registered by inspecting a specific scene, making an illustrated journal entry and map drawing; a **separate** environmental puzzle at that location may still be wholly unsolved. An unrelated letter is proof of its own acquisition, not proof that a whole building is an official shack. A world map mark may appear through exploration or story without conveying any official POI proof. [E02,E03,E05,E06,R15]

**Interaction context:** game controls are **prompt- and platform-sensitive**. Never teach one controller button as universal; focus/inspect/read/equip/loot can have overlapping inputs. D07 owns their detailed mapping once delivered. When no Inspect appears, do not assume the player has failed: step back/adjust vantage, get off horse if safe, approach another side, ensure the relevant object is in view, or consider that the site is scenic rather than registered. Some POIs have unusual vantage points, which may be required for sketch triggering. [E02,E05,E08]

## 2. System families and what actually advances

### S23.M01 — shacks and interiors

“Shack” has **two meanings**: a loose player label for any isolated building, and the game's **specific Total Completion → Miscellaneous → Shacks** category. The latter needs **five qualifying finds**, but there are more than five structures and many ordinary cabins may not advance the counter. PowerPyx catalogues **19** qualifying sites on its 2018 guide and flags certain structures as story-built later; do **not** assume all 19 exist immediately in Chapter 2 or that 19 is a universal exhaustive world-cabin count. [E01,E04,E07]

Inside an accessible shack, environment may communicate through furniture, bodies, damage, books, pictures, sound, notes, loot and staging. Discovering the shack may satisfy one counted site while its **separate contents** remain unexamined. For official credit, inspect **Pause → Progress → Total Completion → Miscellaneous → Shacks** after entering/visiting. Third-party guides differ on whether entry alone or additional investigation is the minimal trigger; use **observed counter** as final evidence rather than inventing an “inspect five clues” rule. [E01,E04,E07,D26-X02]

A shack can be:
- **Empty/scenic** (no confirmed checkmark), **counted official Shack**, **ambient encounter location** (D21), **authored Stranger/mission venue** (D18/Mission Control), **rare-item source** (D22), or **puzzle site** (D26).
- **Persistent/revisitable** geographically yet **finite** in its initial scene, document, unique reward or actor. Reentering a building is not proof a used-up object respawned.
- **Initially unavailable/locked**, later changing with story or time; don't assert that every window is open after Chapter 1. [E04,E07,E13,R08]

### S23.M02 — Points of Interest (POIs), landmark observations and sketches

A **registered POI** is a specific observation: the player finds the eligible scene/object, gets **Inspect/Examine**, and Arthur or John makes a **Journal sketch and often a drawn map icon**. RDR2.org's POI guide lists **50**; a separate RDR2.org landmarks article states **49**; GameFAQs and Red Dead Wiki corroborate the one-POI achievement threshold, **not one invariant exhaustive roster**. For personal all-POI completion, treat exact category/version/aliases as disputed until a consistent checklist is reconciled against actual journal sketches. [E02,E03,E05,E08,D26-X01]

**A POI is not a generic map icon**, a discovered town, a random stranger marker, a dinosaur bone mailing item, a cigarette card, or every structure worth exploring. Certain artifacts are both interesting sites and separate collectible mechanics; only their **correct** evidence surface counts. [E02,E03,R15,R20]

**POI workflow:** recognize a distinctive feature without a map-spoiler search → look for the exact Inspect target → let journal sketch finish → if tracking official 100%, check the **Collectibles → Point of Interest** row → if personally exhaustive, record the exact journal entry separately. Don't claim the same discovery satisfied other collectible chains. [E01,E02,E03,E05]

### S23.M03 — environmental clues, puzzles, secrets and speculative lore

Clues may be **written** (a note, inscription, book, map or poster), **visual** (finger count, alignment, painted image), **spatial** (landmark relationships), **temporal** (night/time/weather/visit conditions), or **state-dependent** (scene evolves following story or revisit). They can lead to:
- An **observable mechanical solution** (the game clearly changes an object, triggers sound/scene or reveals a reward).
- A **quest/Stranger continuation** (D18 owns actual mission progression).
- A **collectible hand-in** (D20 owns set rules and submission).
- A **rare named item** (D22 owns possession/missability).
- **Pure environmental interpretation**, which may have no quest counter, prize, codified answer or explicit ending. [E09,E10,E11,E12]

Document **what is on screen** and what the player infers separately. Avoid saying a player “completed” an unsolved narrative mystery merely because a particular scene or note was found.

### S23.M04 — journal, letters, sketches and document continuity

Arthur's journal is both **story narration** and a durable illustrated record of some POIs and experiences. Not every conversation or book gets a full sketch; each entry is **not** a separate task. Distinguish the journal page, a physical letter in Satchel/Documents, a map annotation, and a Compendium entry. The journal can include actor-dependent/art-style differences during normal later story progression, so a screenshot of a later journal page does not prove who first visited the site. [E03,E06,E14,R15]

**Reading is not retaining:** some interactable books and writing are inspected in place; others may be picked up and re-read. Before leaving a limited interior, check the exact object prompt and whether the intended document is actually in Documents. Protect named items as **D22**, not by assuming every journal entry has a detachable paper. [R22,E06]

## 5. Time, resources, risks, recoverability and preservation

| Exploration circumstance | Hazard class / reversibility | Risk and benefit | Practical default, not a forced quest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passing an ordinary scenic landmark | **REVISITABLE / LOW** | May hide interesting scenery; no confirmed one-off reward | Notice and move on if nothing actionable; no map-clearing compulsion |
| Near a **known POI Inspect prompt** | **OPPORTUNITY / usually deferrable** | Journal sketch and official 1-POI credit if not yet earned | If convenient, inspect now; check journal and criterion separately |
| Entering a **qualifying shack** | **GEOGRAPHY usually revisitable**, *contents possibly finite* | Counts toward five only if game registers; notes/loot may differ | Look around, read optional details and verify shack row; protect named items before exit |
| Entering a dangerous/unfamiliar interior | **RISK / ambush, bear, hostile owner, wanted** | Injury or Honor/bounty change may matter | Scan before forcing entry; don't loot/attack to satisfy an imagined requirement; D21/D13 handle combat/law |
| Finding a named letter/clue in a changing scene | **CONDITIONAL one-time content** | Text/reward may be lost by a choice/chapter or actor change | Read/copy exact object **while legitimately available**; verify retained Document separately |
| A visual puzzle with movable buttons or staged objects | **STATEFUL puzzle; reset/revisit item-specific** | Triggered result may differ from journal inspection | Observe and optionally photograph/describe clue; reveal hint before answer; claim physical reward separately |
| Camp or Story sequence changing locations | **CHAPTER/ACTOR CONDITIONAL** | Certain contextual scenes and annotations may not recur | If a specific rare scene is *observed*, examine it before story progression; D08 determines actual urgent boundaries |
| Rumored secret inaccessible through normal play | **DISPUTED / EXPLOIT-DEPENDENT** | Some web posts offer glitches or “hidden endings” | Do not promise an objective or require exploits. Research independently, protect organic play |
| A mystery with no observable solution state | **INTERPRETIVE, not finite puzzle** | Delivers lore satisfaction without a “complete” marker | Record evidence and multiple hypotheses as theories; leave unresolved when the game does |

**Finite evidence ≠ globally finite location:** a physical cabin can remain while its NPC, newspaper, letter, body position or quest stage changes. Conversely a single entry in a journal can remain while world props disappear. **Only classify a specific object as HARD MISSABLE when strong item-level evidence establishes a one-time window and no verified normal return**; else use CONDITIONAL or DISPUTED. [D26-C15,R21,R22]

**Cost model:** mostly time, weather, travel safety, consumables, carrying slots and potential wanted/Honor consequences; some puzzles yield money, but a drawn clue **does not mean cash/items earned**. D22 governs sell/retain trade-offs, D20 formal collectible turn-ins; don't invent map payout or personally purchased equipment.

## 6. Repeatability, missability and recorded proof classes

A systematic audit should classify **the particular experience/object**, not an entire region. [D26-C04,C15,C16]

| Classification | Criterion | Examples without future plot details | How to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PERSISTENT / REVISITABLE** | Place can normally be physically entered again in allowed world region | Most fixed landscapes, ordinary roadside buildings | Visited when player reports; not “inspected” by inference |
| **REGISTERABLE ONE TIME** | POI sketch/Compendium-type observation normally registered once, while world remains | POI journal scene and etched map mark | Register exact name and visible journal result once; do not keep “farming” |
| **CONDITIONAL / STAGED** | Different condition, revisit, time, story or actor displays something different | Certain evolving interior paintings/sights; some hour-specific effects | Record condition and observed stage, without inventing guaranteed next trigger |
| **ONE-TIME REWARD / DOCUMENT** | A named letter/object awarded or picked up in finite scene | Specific rare item or note, if supported individually | Consult D22; separate seen, picked up, retained, saved |
| **CONFIRMED FINITE ENCOUNTER** | NPC/world event has limited instances/outcomes | Specific player-observed one-off human encounter | **D21 primary**; D26 records only its environmental evidence |
| **DISPUTED / RUMOR** | Missing canonical interaction, ambiguous source, claims of external hidden mechanism | Alleged guaranteed “summoning” at ritual site | Keep theoretical, do not create missing-object checklist |
| **EDITION/PLATFORM INAPPLICABLE** | Content unavailable in player's install/edition, not a missed pickup | Some postlaunch Story content differences | Check version/menu rather than log “missed” |

### Observation confidence ladder

**A. Player/screenshot verified**: specific actual in-game prompt/Journal/Total Completion/Document on current file. **B. Independently corroborated general rule**: multiple reliable guide sources with reproducible menu or mechanics. **C. Specialist single-source**: plausible but not independently tested. **D. Theory, inferred implication, rumor**: **not** accepted as canon. **UNKNOWN**: no applicable evidence.

The **research dossier** can identify B/C/D; it **cannot** create A for the player's save. This explicitly prevents the Companion or GameGuide from writing “five shacks discovered” because the player read the relevant instructions.

### Spoiler/urgency priority

- **NOW**: only when a *specific, currently observed* finite interaction would disappear on leaving/advancing, supported by relevant D21/D22/D08 research or clear game cue.
- **BEFORE EXIT**: chapter-limited clue or optional quest stage actually activated; confirm condition, not guessed timing.
- **CAN WAIT**: generic POI and shack quota, later-region exploration, untriggered curiosity.
- **DISPUTED**: contested missability/secret ritual; don't transform uncertainty into a flashing emergency.

**Anecdotes that “one chance to sketch every mystery” is required are not supported.** The official one-POI/five-shack completion subset remains reachable via many places. [E01,E04]

## 8. Three organic Chapter 2 examples — T0/T1, no solution spoilers

### Scenario A — A strange, marked rock while riding the Heartlands

**Player says:** “I spotted writing on a rock on the way back to Horseshoe. What's this?”  
**Guide behavior:**
1. **Don't reveal a complete POI route or secret backstory.** Explain that the game has inspectable landmarks and environmental signs; some trigger a journal sketch.
2. If the rock really shows a contextual **Inspect** prompt, encourage examining it without needing a mission marker or collectible spreadsheet.
3. After the interaction, open **Journal** to see whether a drawing or annotation was made. If the player wants official credit, open **Total Completion → Collectibles → Point of Interest** and check the actual change.
4. If no sketch appears, mark **visited/observed only** and ask for a view of the prompt or approach angle rather than inventing a result.
5. Return naturally to the outing: the rock need not explain a full mystery.

**Mechanic lesson:** **visiting ≠ inspecting ≠ registering**. A real Heartlands example is **Register Rock**; RDR2.org separately documents it as an inspectable landmark, but this scenario does not assume the player found that exact rock. [E02,E05]

### Scenario B — An unmarked cabin with a note

**Player says:** “There's an abandoned cabin. I found a note and a strange painting.”  
**Guide behavior:**
1. Let the user enjoy the find; offer to **read the note without interpreting it yet** and examine whether the painting has an interaction.
2. Distinguish actual **shack counter** from mere entry: if user tracks official completion, look at **Miscellaneous → Shacks** before/after. Not all interiors are in the qualifying list; no automatic “1/5” claim.
3. If a named document is pickupable, identify whether the game actually placed it in **Satchel/Documents**. Do not advise selling an unknown named object.
4. If the scene appears to change on repeat visits, record only the two observed stages (not a proven supernatural explanation or a timer).
5. Leave whenever curiosity is satisfied; an unresolved cabin is **not an unfinished mandatory quest**.

**Mechanic lesson:** **investigating vs official shack credit vs preserving a separate document**. No particular cabin, note or changing painting is claimed to have appeared in the player's session. [E04,E06,R22]

### Scenario C — Tracks lead to a burnt site and an odd object

**Player says:** “I followed a trail and found a burnt wagon and scattered things. Is this a quest?”  
**Guide behavior:**
1. First classify visible interaction: **Inspect** for a landmark, **Take/Read** for a named note, **Loot** for separate goods, or merely a set-dressing clue. Don't assume every environmental scene has a mission.
2. If a recognized POI inspect is present, complete that specific observation and check journal; **do not** assume nearby lockbox contents were collected.
3. If no quest marker/confirmation exists, describe as **environmental storytelling/possibly POI**, not “mission completed.”
4. Unless a D21 finite encounter or named D22 one-time object is actually present, no time-critical warning. Continue exploring or return to camp as desired.

**Mechanic lesson:** one scene can contain **location, sketch, document and loot** with separate evidence. The New Hanover **Brush Fire** POI is a independently described analogue; no exact hidden loot/coordinates/solution is provided here. [E02,E05]

These scenarios represent **paper research examples**, not in-game tests or actual player observations.

- **New Austin geography / epilogue access:** Some POIs and shacks in broad lists lie in later safe-access areas; early walkthroughs may present them beside Chapter 2 sites without respecting normal progression. Treat as **DEFER / LATER REGION**, not an early missable deadline; don't recommend out-of-bounds exploits for exploration. [E02,E04]
- **Arthur/John journal art:** Known artistic differences and actor-specific annotations are documented, but the existence of alternate drawings **does not establish that every exact version is normally earnable in one playthrough**. Don't require duplication of all actor sketches to meet official 100%. [E06,R22]
- **Temporary chapter-specific scenes:** A place may change due to a story event or authored Stranger/encounter state. Missing a variation can affect **personal exhaustive storytelling**, not the one-POI/five-shack official threshold; don't list names in T0. D08/D21 govern actual time-limited windows. [E14,R08]
- **Hidden environmental letters and physical unique rewards:** a named document/component can be one-time even where geography persists. D22 owns item-specific hard/conditional missability and the save/compendium split; do not extrapolate universal recoverability or nonrecoverability. [R22]

### 9C. Hypotheses versus canon: explicit epistemic standard

1. **Observed canonical artifact/scene:** screen shows a painting, note, statue or anomalous effect; game interaction is reproducible within described limits.
2. **Game text as character testimony:** quote/paraphrase the *character's in-world belief* as such, **not** established truth of universe.
3. **Community interpretation:** label “theory”; give its observed supporting clues and competing explanations. An external mythological/religious parallel isn't a publisher announcement of intent.
4. **Unsupported rumor/exploit:** mark unverified; never promise a hidden journal, third meteorite, guaranteed UFO reward, ritual unlock or missing achievement without primary/properly reproduced evidence.
5. **Mystery unresolved:** record “no confirmed canonical explanation,” rather than inventing a solution.

**No mythology completion counter exists in the official Total Completion checklist.** “Investigate thoroughly” here means one can inspect and interpret, not compel supernatural answers.
